## The thing that is actually broken

Autopilot's promise is *"send us your photos and video, we handle the rest:
copy, layout, SEO, and launch. No waiting months on content."*

That last sentence is a claim about **their** behaviour, not ours. And it is the
claim most likely to fail, because tradesmen do not send photos. Not because
they are difficult, but because "send me photos of your work" is a vague,
open-ended, low-urgency ask landing on someone who is on a roof.

Read that against the unit economics in `BUSINESS_MODEL.md`:

- Autopilot is ~18 to 20 build hours against a $2,994 six-month minimum
- **Month 7 is where the client becomes profitable**

A build that stalls for six weeks waiting on a camera roll does not delay the
invoice, but it does burn the term. The client pays for weeks in which nothing
visible happened, which is the exact shape of a month-7 cancellation. So the
photo bottleneck is not an annoyance. **It is the main threat to the margin**,
and it is the thing a package should be designed to remove.

That reframes "photos marketing" from a nice upsell into the highest-leverage
product on this list.

## The three packages, in the order they should be built

### 1. Photo Rescue, $295 one-time

**The pitch:** "Don't take new photos. Send me the ones you already have."

Every trade business has 200 to 2,000 job photos sitting in a phone, taken for
insurance, for a customer, or for a quote. They are unsorted, badly lit,
sideways, and full of duplicates. The owner thinks they are unusable. Most of
them are, but forty of them are not, and forty is a whole website.

What they do: share the album. That is the entire ask, and it takes ninety
seconds instead of six weeks.

What we do: cull to the best 40, straighten and crop, colour-correct, convert
to WebP, write alt text and captions, and place them.

**Why this is the right first product:**
- It removes the delivery blocker instead of pricing around it
- The asset already exists, so there is nothing for the client to schedule
- It is almost all our time and no third-party cost
- It is the natural thing to offer on the discovery call when they say
  "I don't really have any good pictures"

**Cost to deliver:** 3 to 4 hours. At the $100/hr effective rate that is thin at
$295, so it stops being a standalone product and becomes what it should be:
**a reason to sign, bundled free into a 12-month Autopilot commitment.** That
trade is worth making. A longer term moves the profitability date and removes
the delivery risk in the same stroke.

### 2. Shoot Day, $895 one-time

A half day on a job site or at their shop. 60 to 80 finished images plus short
vertical video clips for Google Business Profile and social.

**Reality check before selling this:** the best trade photography is *work
happening*, which means being on site during a job, which means their schedule
and weather, not ours. It does not scale with a solo operator and it competes
directly with build hours, which are the margin.

**So: subcontract it.** A Boise commercial photographer is roughly $400 to $600
for a half day. At $895 that is a real margin for coordination and editing,
with none of Boyd's calendar consumed. Do not deliver this personally beyond
the first one or two, which exist to learn what to brief.

Sell it to Momentum clients and to anyone whose Photo Rescue came back thin.

### 3. Launch Content Sprint, included, not sold

Not a package. A change to how onboarding runs.

The project brief below **is** this product. It converts an open-ended "send me
stuff" into a bounded task with a deadline, which is the difference between a
two-week build and a two-month one.

Give it a name on the call. "I'll send you the project brief, it takes about
twenty minutes, and once it's back I start building." A named artifact with a
stated duration gets done. An email asking for photos does not.

## "How much are they investing"

Read this as qualification, and note that money is the *least* useful of the
three things worth qualifying on:

| What to qualify | Why it matters | How to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Money** | Table stakes. $499/mo or not. | Already in the call guide |
| **Time** | The real predictor. Twenty minutes of brief in week one predicts everything. | "Who's going to be my point of contact, and can they give me twenty minutes this week?" |
| **Decision** | Trades often have a silent partner or a spouse who decides. | "Is there anyone else who'd want a say before we start?" |

**The project brief is the qualification instrument.** Somebody who returns it
inside a week is a good client. Somebody who needs three chases will need three
chases for everything, forever, and their build will eat the margin.

That makes it worth sending *before* the agreement rather than after. It costs
nothing to send, and a prospect who will not spend twenty minutes on their own
business before paying will not spend it after.

## What to do about "solve problems for tradesmen"

The current positioning sells *deliverables* (a site, an assistant, blog posts).
Trades do not buy deliverables, they buy the removal of a specific irritation.
Same product, different first sentence:

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Custom website" | "You stop losing the jobs that Google gives to someone else" |
| "AI receptionist" | "Nobody who calls at 8pm goes to a competitor" |
| "4 blog posts a month" | "You show up when someone searches your trade plus your town" |
| "Monthly report" | "You find out which jobs came from the website" |
| "Custom software" | "Quote it in two minutes on your phone instead of an hour at the kitchen table" |

That last one is the Momentum pitch and it is by far the strongest, because it
is the only one no competitor in this market can say. **Phoenix Stoneworks is
the proof**, and a case study with real numbers is worth more than any of this
copy. It is still the biggest gap on rubyxqube.com.

## On the word "AI"

Manny asked for it removed from his site, and his instinct matches the
direction already recorded in the repositioning notes: the "websites plus AI
chatbots" pitch is being retired anyway.

Worth deciding deliberately rather than by drift, because the two sites have
opposite audiences:

- **On a client site**, "AI" is a liability. A homeowner wants to know somebody
  will call them back. Done: PSW now says "virtual assistant" throughout.
- **On rubyxqube.com**, "AI" is still how a prospect understands what they are
  buying, and it is a search term they type. Removing it there is a real
  positioning decision with a real cost.

**Resolved 2026-08-25.** Boyd's call: soften, do not remove. "AI receptionist"
became "digital receptionist" as the product NAME across the marketing pages,
with AI kept as the EXPLANATION next to it, so a prospect reads the outcome
first and the technology second. Pricing now says "Digital receptionist, AI
trained on your business".

Three things stayed deliberately: the blog, which targets "AI receptionist" as
a search term and includes a post titled after it; Privacy and Terms, where
precision beats softness; and the `/ai-receptionist` URL, which earns inbound
and which nobody feels sold to by.
